# NLP Models for Climate Policy Analysis Part 1: Evidence Synthesis

## About this tutorial

Climate-policy decisions depend on reliable scientific evidence. However, the volume of climate-related research has grown so rapidly that manually identifying, screening, and organising all relevant studies can be difficult and time-consuming.

This hands-on tutorial introduces participants to using natural language processing and supervised machine learning for climate evidence synthesis. Participants will work with academic literature data, convert text into machine-readable representations, train classification models, and evaluate how effectively the models identify relevant climate studies.

The tutorial also explores how NLP can support systematic evidence mapping while emphasising that automated classification should complement, rather than replace, expert human review.

### Tutorial creators
- Daniel Spokoyny, Carnegie Mellon University, dspokoyn@cs.cmu.edu
- Max Callaghan, Mercator Research Institute on Global Commons and Climate - Berlin, callaghan@mcc-berlin.net
- Tobias Schimanski, University of Zurich, tobias.schimanski@df.uzh.ch


### Learning objectives
By the end of this tutorial, participants should be able to:

- Explain the role of evidence synthesis in climate-policy research.
- Describe how NLP can support literature screening and evidence mapping.
- Load and explore academic-publication data.
- Convert text into machine-readable features using word counts and TF-IDF.
- Train a supervised text-classification model.
- Apply a pretrained transformer model to climate-related text.
- Evaluate model performance using precision, recall, accuracy, and F1 score.
- Inspect classification errors and identify limitations of automated evidence screening.

# NLP Models for Climate Policy Analysis Part 2: Prompts for Sustainable Development Goals

## About this tutorial

Climate-policy organisations produce large numbers of reports, strategies, plans, laws, submissions, and other text-based documents. Reviewing these materials manually can be difficult to scale, particularly when researchers need to compare documents across countries, sectors, institutions, or time periods.

This tutorial introduces participants to using NLP and language models to classify and analyse climate-policy documents.

Participants will work through a practical policy-document analysis workflow, apply a model to policy text, examine the resulting classifications, and assess whether the outputs are reliable and useful. The tutorial also considers language coverage, contextual interpretation, bias, transparency, and the need for human oversight.

## Tutorial creators
- Daniel Spokoyny, Carnegie Mellon University, dspokoyn@cs.cmu.edu
- Max Callaghan, Mercator Research Institute on Global Commons and Climate - Berlin, callaghan@mcc-berlin.net
- Tobias Schimanski, University of Zurich, tobias.schimanski@df.uzh.ch

## Learning objectives
By the end of this tutorial, participants should be able to:

- Explain how NLP can support climate-policy analysis.
- Prepare policy documents for automated text analysis.
- Apply a language model or text classifier to climate-policy content.
- Interpret model-generated labels or categories.
- Evaluate the usefulness and reliability of the outputs.
- Identify possible sources of bias and misclassification.
- Explain why policy context and human oversight remain important.
- Recognise appropriate and inappropriate uses of automated policy classification.
