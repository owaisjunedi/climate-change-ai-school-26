### Key takeaways from today's lecture:


- AI can support multiple stages of the materials discovery pipeline, from predicting material properties and identifying promising candidates to characterization, optimization, and experimental validation.



- Machine learning can significantly expand how we explore large chemical and materials spaces, using representations of materials and learned latent representations to identify patterns and candidates that would be difficult to search through conventional approaches alone.



- Generative AI and inverse design can shift materials research from predicting the properties of known materials toward designing new materials with desired properties, opening opportunities for faster discovery of climate-relevant materials.



- AI can increasingly be combined with autonomous laboratories and experimental workflows, creating iterative cycles in which models propose candidates, experiments generate new evidence, and the resulting data are used to improve subsequent predictions and decisions.



- Accelerating discovery is not enough on its own. Climate impact depends on connecting AI methods to real materials challenges, such as photovoltaics, batteries, alternative fuels and CO₂ capture, while considering validation, lifecycle impacts, toxicity, critical-mineral use, data and computing access, laboratory safety, and deployment beyond the laboratory.
