### Key takeaways from today's lecture:





- How artificial intelligence and machine learning can be leveraged to monitor, understand, and protect global biodiversity amid growing environmental pressures



- AI models automate multi-modal species identification and tracking using camera traps (e.g., MegaDetector), passive acoustic monitoring (e.g., BirdNET), environmental DNA (eDNA), and drone/satellite remote sensing.



- Correlative and mechanistic SDMs predict species distributions using environmental covariates. Key modeling obstacles include presence-only data, spatial sampling biases from citizen science, and non-causal correlations.



- Satellite metrics (e.g., NDVI) and machine learning models track larger ecological mechanisms, such as invasive species dynamics, plant functional traits, and seasonal phenology shifts.



- AI algorithms like CAPTAIN apply reinforcement learning to optimize conservation planning and protected area selection. Responsible deployment requires adhering to governance frameworks like FAIR and CARE.
