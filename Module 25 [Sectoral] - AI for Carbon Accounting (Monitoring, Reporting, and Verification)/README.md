### A few key takeaways from the session:





1. Bottom-up and top-down carbon-accounting approaches provide different but complementary information. Bottom-up approaches rely on activity data and emission factors, while atmospheric and satellite observations can provide an independent perspective for monitoring and verification.



2. Remote sensing can support carbon accounting at different spatial scales, from broader regional estimates to facility-scale methane and CO₂ plume detection.



3. AI can support several stages of carbon accounting, including activity-data generation, infrastructure identification, plume detection and segmentation, and faster emissions-monitoring workflows.



4. Ground truth and validation remain major challenges. Reliable labels are limited in several stages of the emissions-processing pipeline, and models may not generalize equally well across sensors, regions, and environmental conditions.



5. Trustworthy AI-based MRV requires more than good model accuracy. Uncertainty, explainability, false-positive/false-negative trade-offs, physics-based checks, institutional trust, and the consequences of decisions based on model outputs all matter.
